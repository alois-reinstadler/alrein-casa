How it goes

Four steps, no surprises.

Most of what goes wrong on a web project goes wrong before anyone writes code. So the first two steps are the ones I spend the most time on.

01

A call

Thirty minutes, free, no deck. You tell me what the business actually needs. If I'm not the right person, I'll say so and point you somewhere better.

02

A written proposal

Scope, timeline and a fixed price, on one page. Nothing starts until you've read it and agreed to it.

03

The build

You see working pages every week, on a real URL, not screenshots. Feedback goes in as we go rather than piling up at the end.

04

Launch and handover

I ship it, watch it for a fortnight, and leave you with documentation and a recording of how to run it.

Questions

The ones I get asked most.

Am I really working with one person?

Yes. You talk to me, I do the work, and nothing gets passed to an account manager or offshored. When a project genuinely needs a specialist — a photographer, a copywriter, a translator — I'll tell you and bring one in openly.

What does a website cost?

It depends on how many pages, how much content exists, and whether you need a CMS. I give you a fixed number after the first call rather than a range on a pricing page that would be wrong for you either way.

How long does it take?

Four to eight weeks for most projects. The variable is almost never the build — it's how quickly content and feedback come back from your side.

Do you do design as well as development?

Both. That's most of the point of hiring one person: nothing gets lost in the handoff between a designer who doesn't build and a developer who didn't design.

What happens after launch?

Two weeks of watching and fixing are included. After that you can take it in-house with the documentation I leave you, or put me on a retainer.

Who owns the code?

You do, on final payment. Your repository, your hosting account, your domain. Nothing is held hostage and there's no proprietary platform you have to stay on.
